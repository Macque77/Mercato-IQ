#### Mandatory Foreign-Press Sweep for England Targets

When an English club is linked with a player:
1. **Search Italian press** (current club's home market if Italian)
   - Gianluca Di Marzio, Tuttomercatoweb, Calciomercato, club-specific local beats
2. **Search Spanish press** (La Liga target)
   - Relevo, Marca, AS, local beats (Diario de Navarra, Faro de Vigo, etc.)
3. **Search French press** (Ligue 1 target)
   - L'Equipe, RMC Sport, Foot Mercato, local beats
4. **Search German press** (Bundesliga target)
   - Sky Sport Germany (Plettenberg), Bild, Kicker
5. **Search Portuguese press** (Primeira Liga target)
   - A Bola, Record, O Jogo
6. **Search Benelux press** (Eredivisie/Belgian target)
   - Voetbal International, De Telegraaf, Sacha Tavolieri

## Implementation Guidelines

### Refresh Protocol

1. **Every refresh, follow this order by nation:**
   - Check Tier 1 sources first (official press, major institutional outlets)
   - Query Tier 2 club correspondents and national desks
   - Trace Tier 3 aggregations back to original reporters
   - Run mandatory foreign-press sweep for each nation

2. **Foreign-Press Sweep Checklist**
   - For every linked player, search their **current club's local press in the native language**
   - For every club profile, sweep selling markets the club habitually buys from
   - Trace aggregator links (Sport Witness, Football Italia, TransferFeed) to the original outlet
   - Prioritize local beat reporters within their specialism (tier rises)
   - Document the original source, not the translation/aggregator

3. **Source Quality Over Volume**
   - Weight Tier 1 single-source verification over 5 Tier 3 sources saying the same thing
   - A Romano confirmation outweighs 10 fan accounts
   - A club correspondent carries more weight than a national sports desk for that club's business
   - Local press in the player's current-club market often breaks the story first

4. **Probability Scoring**
   - 90-100%: Tier 1 confirmation (Ornstein, Romano "here we go", official club statement)
   - 75-90%: Multiple Tier 2 sources OR single Tier 1 without full "here we go"
   - 65-75%: Tier 2 single source + supporting secondary evidence
   - 50-65%: Emerging story (Tier 3 + Tier 2, or multi-Tier 3)
   - 30-50%: Single Tier 3 or unconfirmed Tier 2
   - 0-30%: Anonymous ITK, single-fan source, or highly speculative

5. **X/Twitter Monitoring**
   - Follow all listed Tier 1 & Tier 2 reporters
   - Set alerts for names when they appear in threads
   - Use nation + "transfer" hashtag searches daily
   - Cross-check reporter timelines for corroboration

6. **Reporting Standards (per Premier League Transfer Sources v1.4)**
   - **Write in British English** (favour, not favor; match, not game)
   - **No em dashes** (—) anywhere; use commas, colons, or plain hyphens
   - **Journalistic tone** (lead with news, not throat-clearing; short, direct sentences)
   - **Editorial scepticism on thin sourcing:** When a rumour rests on a single Tier 3 source or anonymous ITK, state it plainly in the note. Don't present it neutrally. Skew the language toward scepticism.

7. **Confirmed Transfer Gate**
   - Do not add to CONFIRMED_IN/CONFIRMED_OUT until FootballTransfers.com lists the deal
   - Once confirmed there, remove instantly from INCOMING/OUTGOING/WATCHLIST in the same refresh
   - A player appears exactly once, in exactly one table

## Deferred: How to Use This Guide

1. **Assign reporters to refresh cycles** — each nation gets its Tier 1-2 sweep every 4-6 hours during active transfer windows
2. **Build alerts into your monitoring stack** — subscribe to nation-specific Twitter/X lists for each Tier 1-2 reporter
3. **Store foreign-press sources locally** — build local bookmarks for each nation's key outlets
4. **Weight by specialism** — when a local beat reporter covers their own club, they outrank national journalists
5. **Trace every aggregation back** — never cite TransferFeed, Calciomercato aggregation, or 90min; find and link the original source
6. **Document chain of custody** — track which reporter broke a story first, which second; note when it moves from Tier 3 to Tier 2 confirmation
