Club Position

04

The lens through which every rumour is weighted: the recruitment philosophy, the balance sheet, the brand, and the European trade-off.

The Model CORE PRINCIPLE

An owner-driven, collaborative model under Evangelos Marinakis, with head coach Pereira identifying needs and recruitment drawing up targets. After a £200m, 13-signing spree last summer, this window is about refinement, not volume.

Pereira wants at least two options in every position and three or four signings, "minor tweaks rather than an overhaul", built around a core he believes underachieved.

This window's logic: keep the spine, fund additions through an Anderson sale, and add a forward, wing pace and a centre-back.

  • Likely exit: Elliot Anderson (Man City favourites; Man Utd keen).
  • Forward target linked: a new frontman (Mateta among the names, ~£25m).
  • Markets: targeted quality, no repeat of last summer's volume spree.
Tracker implication: weight a marquee outgoing (Anderson) and three or four focused additions; treat the core as retained.

Financial Position STRONG

Backed by Marinakis, Forest spend ambitiously but must manage the cost rules carefully after heavy recent investment and a drop down the table.

  • No European football brings the looser 85% cost cap.
  • An Anderson sale (£100m-plus) would create major headroom.
  • A drop in finishing position costs prize money, sharpening the need to trade well.
Caveat: the Elanga sale and others sit in the multi-year SCR window; trading remains central.

Reputation & The Cost-Cap Trade-Off

05

Forest retain a strong core and an ambitious owner, but the chaotic season and no European football mean a recalibration, funded by a marquee sale.

Reputational Standing

Forest's reputation took a hit from a season of upheaval, but the core squad and Marinakis' ambition keep them an attractive proposition; Pereira brings needed stability.

  • A talented core (Gibbs-White, Murillo) anchors the rebuild.
  • Marinakis' willingness to spend appeals, within the rules.
Double edge: prized assets like Anderson draw elite bids that both fund and test the rebuild.

The SCR Tightening KEY TENSION

The new Squad Cost Ratio caps wages, agent fees and amortisation at 85% of revenue for clubs outside Europe, the looser of the two limits (European clubs sit at 70%). No continental football is a sporting blow but a quiet financial edge over rivals juggling the tighter cap.

An Anderson sale would ease the ratio significantly; without it, Forest must trade carefully.

  • A 3-year net transfer position feeds the calculation; the Elanga fee sits within it.
  • A marquee sale unlocks the planned additions.
▲ Reward, a focused rebuild off a strong core
  • Keeping the spine and adding three or four could lift Forest to the top half.
  • An Anderson sale funds quality without overspending.
  • Pereira's stability ends the managerial chaos.
▼ Risk, a damaging sell-off
  • Losing Anderson weakens midfield even if it funds others.
  • If the core frays, the rebuild stalls.
  • Lost prize money tightens the budget.
Net read: stabilise under Pereira, keep the core, cash in on Anderson at a premium, and reinvest in three or four targeted signings (a forward, wing pace, a centre-back) within the 85% cap.
